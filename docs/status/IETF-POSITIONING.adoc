= IETF Positioning — where each draft goes, and why
:toc: preamble

*Assessed 2026-08-07* against the live IETF record. Every claim below was read from the
primary source (draft text or datatracker), not from a summary. Supersedes the assumption
that these two drafts share a submission path.

== The finding in one line

The two drafts need *different venues*, because the IETF working group that owns half this
territory has explicitly and repeatedly declined to own the other half.

== What is actually happening at the IETF

The *AIPREF* working group (WIT area; chairs Mark Nottingham, Suresh Krishnan) is chartered
to standardise "the expression of preferences about how content is collected and processed
for AI", attached "using Well-Known URIs (RFC 8615) ... and HTTP response header fields".
Milestones: *31 August 2026*.

[cols="2,1,4",options="header"]
|===
| Document | State | What it means for us

| `draft-ietf-aipref-vocab-06`
| *Active*, Standards Track
| Two categories only — `train-ai`, `search`. Binary `y`/`n`/absent. No conditions, no
  rationale, no path scoping. Serialised as RFC 9651 Structured Fields, *not JSON*.

| `draft-ietf-aipref-attach-04`
| *EXPIRED* 2025-10-28, *no successor*
| Defined a `Content-Usage` HTTP header and a path-specific robots.txt rule. *No
  well-known URI. No JSON.* The attachment lane is vacant.

| `draft-madhavan-aipref-displaybasedpref-02`
| Candidate for WG adoption
| Individual drafts *do* get adopted here. This is the normal route in.

| `draft-wallace-aipref-grant-binding-01`
| 2026-08-06
| Signed, offline-verifiable JWS credentials granting revocable exceptions to named
  parties. *Directly contests AIBDP's `conditional`* — and does it better.

| `draft-zehta-aipref-exclusions-01`
| 2026-04-22
| *Use*-based public-interest carve-outs, not path-based. AIBDP's path `exceptions` remain
  distinct.

| `draft-reilly-aipref-compliance-00`
| 2026-08-01
| Audit records (AUCR). Explicitly: "Enforcement. Nothing here blocks, throttles, or
  penalizes any party."
|===

=== Two quotations that decide everything

From `vocab-06` §4 — *the vocabulary is not settled*:

____
"Its contents DO NOT REFLECT CONSENSUS of the Working Group either in whole or part ...
published solely as a basis of further discussion."
____

From `vocab-06` §6.6 — *alternative formats are explicitly invited*:

____
"This format is only an exemplary way to represent preferences. The data model described in
Section 3 can be used without this serialization. Any alternative format needs to define the
mapping both from that format to the model used in this document and from the model to the
alternative format ... The mapping does not need to be complete, it only needs to be clear
and unambiguous."
____

== Correcting two earlier assessments

*"AIBDP will be overtaken this month."* Overstated. The vocabulary explicitly lacks
consensus and §6.6 invites alternative formats. The risk is not obsolescence; it is
*misalignment* — AIBDP currently invents a parallel vocabulary instead of mapping to theirs.

*"Submitting an individual `-00` reads as not having done the homework."* Wrong. Four
individual drafts were filed into this space in the last week and one is already an adoption
candidate. An individual draft *is* the route in. What would read badly is an individual
draft that does not reference AIPREF at all — which is what we have today.

== Where the enforcement lane stands

Three separate documents each decline to provide enforcement:

* the AIPREF *charter* — "Technical enforcement of preferences" is out of scope
* `vocab-06` — "Enforcement is not provided by this specification. Preferences do not
  themselves create rights, obligations, or prohibitions."
* `compliance-00` — "Nothing here blocks, throttles, or penalizes any party."

Nothing in the WG's document set defines a status code or refusal semantics. *HTTP 430's
lane is uncontested, and the gap is one the working group has deliberately left open.*

== The blocker that applies regardless

Both drafts declare `category="std"` *and* `submissionType="independent"`. The Independent
Stream cannot publish Standards Track documents or create IANA registry entries, and the
HTTP Status Codes registry is *IETF Review* (RFC 9110 §16.2.1; 430 currently unassigned).

*As written, the 430 draft cannot achieve its own purpose in its declared stream.* This is
independent of the `std` vs `info` question recorded in ADR-0005 — that decision addressed a
second-order variable.

== Recommended positioning

=== HTTP 430 — reframe as enforcement of AIPREF preferences

Strongest available pitch, because it opens on the working group's own words. Make the
normative dependency *AIPREF*, not AIBDP:

____
A server MAY return 430 (Consent Required) when a request conflicts with a declared usage
preference — for example `Content-Usage: train-ai=n`.
____

AIBDP becomes one richer declaration source rather than a hard dependency. This decouples
430's fate from AIBDP's, removes the stale cross-draft references, and answers "why does
this belong at the IETF" before it is asked.

Rename to `draft-jewell-aipref-http-430` to signal alignment. Take it to the AIPREF list
first; venue is then AD sponsorship or HTTPBIS, since AIPREF's charter excludes it.

=== AIBDP — reposition as a mapped alternative format

Do *not* submit as a competing vocabulary. Under §6.6, define the bidirectional mapping:

* `train-ai` ⟷ `policies.training`
* `search` ⟷ `policies.indexing`
* AIBDP purposes absent from AIPREF map to *unknown*, which §6.6 explicitly permits

Honest differential after mapping — narrower than previously claimed:

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
| Still distinct | Note

| Well-known URI + JSON manifest | `attach-04` is expired with no successor and defined no well-known URI. *This is the real opening.*
| Path-based `scope` and `exceptions` | `zehta-exclusions` is use-based; robots.txt path rules are coarser
| `rationale` | No AIPREF equivalent
| Enforcement binding | Belongs to the 430 draft
|===

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
| No longer defensible | Why

| `conditional` as headline feature | `wallace-grant-binding` does this with verifiable JWS credentials; AIBDP uses unverified request headers
| "8 purposes vs 2" | `vocab-06` §4.3: extensions "MUST NOT introduce additional categories that include existing categories" — subsets only. Several AIBDP purposes need re-expressing as subsets or dropping
| Per-path scoping as a differential | AIPREF-*as-a-system* has it via the robots.txt rule in `attach`
|===

== Work this creates

. Define the §6.6 bidirectional mapping. Without it AIBDP is a competing vocabulary; with
  it, a sanctioned alternative format.
. Adopt `vocab-06` §5.1's conflict rule, or define AIBDP's own ordering over
  `{allowed, refused, conditional, encouraged}`. AIBDP currently says "most restrictive
  SHOULD take precedence" with no ordering — `DEBT.adoc` item 2 already admits this.
. Re-express AIBDP purposes as subsets per §4.3, or drop them.
. Decide `conditional`'s future: cede to `wallace-grant-binding`, or justify the
  lighter-weight mechanism. Do not both claim it and leave the crypto section incoherent.
. Rewrite the 430 draft's normative core on the RFC 7725 model, with real RFC 2119 language
  and the §16.2.1 registration template.
. Read the AIPREF list archive for `well-known`, `JSON`, `conditional`, `path`,
  `status code` before pitching — AIPREF's minimalism may be considered consensus rather
  than oversight.

== Decisions for the owner

. *Venue* — take 430 to the AIPREF list then seek AD sponsorship / HTTPBIS? Reposition
  AIBDP as a mapped alternative format?
. *`category` / `submissionType`* — must become coherent. IETF stream + `std`, or
  independent + `info` and abandon the registry ambition.
. *Draft names* — adopt `draft-jewell-aipref-*` to signal alignment?
. *`conditional`* — contest `wallace-grant-binding`, or cede and cite it?
. *Author identity* — unresolved since ADR-0005; determines who submits.
